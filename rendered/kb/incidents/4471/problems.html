The problems

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. A day-first tenant writes 7/4 for 7 April and gets an invite for 4 July. It reaches every tenant through every channel. This is the problem #4471 describes, and the queued fix is for it.

hint: booking-v3’s form tells people to type the month first. It prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, people type what it says, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The symptom is the same swap, so the reports read like the parser’s. The code is different: fixing the parser leaves it as it is. It only comes through the form, only for tenants on booking-v3.

one-day: a date moves by one day. “Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May.” “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting.” A month-first parser swaps day and month; it cannot move a date by one day. A time zone applied at a day boundary can. The desk noticed these (desk-notes.json) and keeps them counted under the parser by tenant. They stay counted there, and have a ticket of their own.

When the parser fix ships, #4471 will look closed. The form hint and the one-day move stay open, and their reports keep arriving with the same subject lines.

Why one ticket held them all

  • The desk merges on subject alone (decision 0001). All of these problems produce the same subjects, “Wrong day on the invite”, “Date is wrong”, so subject matching cannot keep them apart.
  • Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser, not the tenant’s locale and form. The note under the table shows how often it names the wrong problem.
  • The ticket export pages overlap, so the same report can come twice. Count by report id.

For the desk

  • Keep #4471 for the parser. Move the hint and one-day reports to their own tickets, linked in the table.
  • Decision 0001 stays as written. A new record should replace it: merge on cause (tenant, channel, booking form), checked against the report’s words, not on subject.
